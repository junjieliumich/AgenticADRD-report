Misperceived Social Norms

Women Working Outside the Home in Saudi Arabia — a structured comparison of four experiments reported in Bursztyn, González, and Yanagizawa-Drott (American Economic Review, 2020).

10 design features4 experimentsOpen source PDF

Study overview

The paper studies whether Saudi men underestimate support for women working outside the home (WWOH), whether correcting perceived norms changes household labor-supply decisions, and whether the same information affects women’s choices in a natural recruitment setting. This report separates the main experiment, two national-survey designs, and the surveyor recruitment experiment.

Source and extraction scope

Study content was extracted solely from the complete 34-page source PDF. The review covered main text, all footnotes present in the file, tables, figures, captions, references, and all pages after the reference list. The PDF cites online appendices and registry materials, but those materials are not embedded in the supplied file and were not consulted.

Experiment mapping. P1-E1 and P1-E2 use the paper’s headings “Main Experiment” and “First National Survey.” P1-E3 maps the requested randomized wording check to the paper’s “separate online survey” and Figure 8’s label “Second National Survey.” P1-E4 uses the paper’s heading “Surveyor Recruitment Experiment.”

Unresolved gaps and limitations

Source-bounded gaps

The supplied PDF references online Appendix A, Appendix B tables and figures, Appendix C scripts, registry records, and replication data, but does not contain them. Consequently, appendix-only numeric balance results, some robustness details, scripts, and additional heterogeneity evidence remain outside this report’s source boundary.

P1-E3 is described mainly as a survey supporting a correlational mechanism analysis; its randomized wording check appears in Figure 7’s caption and footnote 35, not as a separately titled experiment. The PDF gives the two support percentages but not arm sizes or a formal test of their difference.

Interpretive cautions

Self-reported longer-term outcomes in P1-E1 are subject to follow-up attrition. Potential treatment-to-control communication could attenuate differences. The P1-E3 discussion-frequency relationship is observational and may reflect reverse causality. P1-E4 uses a one-day job, and the paper explicitly leaves effects for longer jobs unresolved.

Extraction-method note

The PDF was converted to page-preserving text and reviewed from beginning to end. Findings were split into atomic component-level claims, organized into the ten requested features, and assigned IDs only after extraction in experiment–feature order. Page locations use PDF page numbers and printed journal pages when available. Exact displayed totals, matrix contents, filters, and evidence views are generated from one embedded finding array.
